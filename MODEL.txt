PIANO MATERIAL LAB - WHAT THE KNOBS ACTUALLY CONTROL

This is a reduced physical model, not a calibrated reconstruction of a real
piano and not a full 3D finite-element or acoustic-field simulation. Every one
of the 42 knobs is connected to the rendered model. The monitor-level slider
is separate and does not pretend to be an instrument property.

STRINGS AND HAMMER
Three slightly detuned strings each carry 32 transverse modes. With length L,
area A, linear density mu=rho*A, tension T, second moment I=pi*d^4/64 and Young's
modulus E, angular modal frequencies are:
  omega_n^2 = (T/mu)*(n*pi/L)^2 + (E*I/mu)*(n*pi/L)^4
Modal masses are mu*L/2. Detuning is implemented as a change in tension. The
TUNE C4 button solves T for 261.625565 Hz using the CURRENT wire dimensions and
material, including bending stiffness. It does not silently retune the string
when you change other physical knobs. Tension must remain in the knob's range.

The hammer is an actual moving mass with position and velocity. Felt force is
F = F_at_1mm * max(compression / 0.001, 0)^exponent. Contact compression depends
on the current string displacement at the strike point, averaged over the
three strings. Equal total-force sharing among the unisons is assumed. Hammer
contact is integrated at 8x the audio rate; the strings retain existing motion
when struck again. A finite contact width applies the spatial integral of each
mode shape. This captures contact-duration and strike-location effects without
using recorded audio or a generic brightness knob.

Damping uses positive modal losses. Higher string modes have a fixed quadratic
increase in intrinsic loss. This model omits longitudinal string motion,
nonlinear tension modulation, separate transverse polarizations, duplex scaling,
and sympathetic vibration of the other 87 notes. These are limitations, not
hidden knobs or claimed effects.

BRIDGE AND SOUNDBOARD
The bridge is a mass-spring-damper transfer system. String endpoint force drives
it; its support force drives the board. A positive driving-point-mobility
estimate also sets the string's radiation-loss rate (bounded at 20/s). This is
a feed-forward body model plus a passive loading estimate, not a fully coupled,
energy-exact bridge/board/string solve.

The board has 64 modes of an orthotropic, simply supported rectangular plate.
Its frequencies follow the thin-plate equation with along-grain modulus,
cross-grain modulus, density, thickness, length and width. Poisson ratio 0.3 and
shear modulus sqrt(Ex*Ey)/5 are fixed assumptions. The bridge sits at fractions
0.37 and 0.43 of the plate dimensions. The bridge footprint spatially averages
modal excitation. The plate model has no explicit ribs, crown, tapered edges,
cutouts, or measured piano response; its lowest mode can be lower than that of
a real braced soundboard. Loss factor controls mode damping.

Radiation uses modal displaced-volume acceleration, with an approximate small
off-axis term for modes that cancel on axis. Microphone angle modifies the
finite-radiator directivity approximation. This is not a full directional
soundboard radiation calculation. The plotted signal is normalized model
pressure, not a calibrated microphone SPL measurement.

CASE AND CAVITY
One effective casing-panel mode has frequency sqrt(K/M)/(2*pi) and a structural
loss factor. The air cavity is a lumped Helmholtz-type approximation:
  omega_H = c*sqrt(aperture_area / (volume*effective_neck_length))
with c=343 m/s and effective neck length 0.06 m + 1.7*sqrt(area/pi). The lid
aperture knob is an equivalent open AREA, not a modeled hinge angle. Case and
cavity radiation mixes have fixed geometric scale factors; their controls change
actual resonance frequencies and decay, not arbitrary effect presets.

DAMPERS
After key release, a first-order lowering motion increases damping at the felt
contact. Contact loss depends on the mean squared mode shape over the damper
width. The point is therefore important: modes near a node can ring longer.
Positions mirrored about the string midpoint have the same diagonal damping
response in this simplified model. Friction, width, position and preload all
enter this calculation. Compression scales contact damping by sqrt(x/0.5 mm),
an explicitly phenomenological approximation. Pedal lift reduces contact;
100% lift leaves the string free after key release. Inter-mode damper coupling,
bounce and detailed felt friction are not resolved.

ROOM AND MICROPHONE
A rectangular room supplies six first-order image-source reflection distances.
The source is centered horizontally; source and microphone are 1.2 m high, with
the microphone offset along the room's length. Wall absorption sets reflected
amplitudes and an Eyring decay estimate: T60 = 0.161 V / (-S ln(1-alpha)). A damped
feedback delay network approximates the diffuse tail. This is not ray tracing
or a measured room impulse response. Microphone distance affects propagation
delay and geometric spreading; polar angle affects radiator directivity.

LIVE CHANGES AND OUTPUT
Structural changes preserve oscillator state and crossfade two model instances
for 2048 samples (~46 ms). Fast drags are coalesced until the preceding fade
finishes. This is an artifact-avoidance mechanism, not a physical simulation of
instantaneously replacing a vibrating piano's material. Hammer properties shape
the next contact. AUTO STRIKE triggers every 2.5 seconds with a one-second hold.

The audio path uses six 512-sample buffers and Windows completion events, with
no Sleep polling. Output is mono 44.1 kHz / 16-bit PCM. A safety ceiling is
transparent below 0.7 full scale and soft-limits extreme experiments above that.
'Safety: LIMITED' records ceiling activity since launch. Reduce monitor level
if needed; it does not alter the physical forces. No native audio hearing or
measured acoustic-piano match is claimed by the coding agent.
